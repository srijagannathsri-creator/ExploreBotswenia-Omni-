Introduction to the Constitution

The Constitution of the Federal Republic of Botswenia is the supreme law of the country. Its official name in Vuluz is "រដ្ឋធម្មនុញ្ញ វ៉ា បោត្សុវេនិអ". It establishes the framework of government, defines the powers and responsibilities of national institutions and provides the foundation for the country's legal and political system.

The Constitution was adopted on 15 April 1953 and came into effect on 16 January 1955. It serves as the foundation of constitutional governance, defining the relationship between the government, national institutions and the people of Botswenia.

It also establishes the principles under which public authority is exercised and ensures that the administration of the country operates within constitutional boundaries.

History and Drafting of the Constitution

The Constitution of the Federal Republic of Botswenia was drafted by Jayabhushan S. Roy and Hiranmoyo Yamataha. Their work established the constitutional foundation of the country's governing and legal institutions.

It was formally adopted on 15 April 1953 and came into force on 16 January 1955. Its establishment laid the foundation for Botswenia's constitutional and parliamentary system of governance.

Since its implementation, the Constitution has served as the principal legal framework for the organisation and functioning of national institutions and the administration of the Federal Republic of Botswenia.

Constitutional Framework and Government Powers

The Constitution defines the structure, authority and responsibilities of the government of Botswenia. It establishes the powers of national institutions and determines the constitutional limits within which those powers may be exercised.

Government authorities and public officials are required to operate within the framework of the Constitution. Their powers are not unlimited, and constitutional provisions establish boundaries for the exercise of public authority.

The constitutional framework provides the legal basis for the functioning of government institutions, public administration and the country's parliamentary system.

People's Powers and Constitutional Rights

The Constitution recognises the people as an essential part of the country's constitutional and democratic system. It establishes the relationship between citizens and the government and defines the rights, protections and responsibilities associated with citizenship.

It also provides a constitutional framework for the people's participation in national governance and establishes limitations on governmental authority to protect constitutional rights.

Through these provisions, the Constitution defines both the powers exercised by public institutions and the constitutional rights and powers held by the people.

Fundamental Rights

Fundamental Rights form an important part of the constitutional framework of the Federal Republic of Botswenia. They provide essential protections and establish constitutional principles concerning individual rights and freedoms.

These rights define important protections that must be respected by government institutions and public authorities. They also help establish constitutional limits on the exercise of governmental power.

The recognition of Fundamental Rights strengthens the relationship between citizens, the government and the constitutional system.

Conclusion

The Constitution of the Federal Republic of Botswenia is the foundation of the country's constitutional, legal and governmental system. Officially known in Vuluz as "រដ្ឋធម្មនុញ្ញ វ៉ា បោត្សុវេនិអ", it provides the framework through which national institutions exercise their authority and fulfil their responsibilities.

It defines the powers and limitations of the government, establishes the constitutional position of the people and provides a framework for the protection of Fundamental Rights. These provisions help ensure that governmental authority operates within established constitutional boundaries.

Adopted in 1953 and brought into effect in 1955, the Constitution remains central to Botswenia's constitutional identity, parliamentary governance and legal administration.
